layout: post
title: Reed's law
law: The utility of large networks, particularly social networks, scales exponentially with the size of the network.
law-author: David P. Reed
law-url: https://hbr.org/2001/02/the-law-of-the-pack
law-year: 2001
---

Reed's law is often contrasted with Metcalfe's law, which holds that the value of a network scales with the square of the number of participants. Reed observed that networks can be used to form groups as well as point-to-point connections, and the number of possible groups grows much faster than the number of point-to-point connections. This is what distinguishes social networks from older networks such as the telephone system: the value comes not just from connecting any two members, but from the near-unlimited variety of subgroups and communities that members can form. The law helps explain the explosive growth of network effects in modern software, where platforms become more valuable - sometimes staggeringly so - as they add users.
