title: Jevons Paradox
law: It is a confusion of ideas to suppose that the economical use of fuel is equivalent to a diminished consumption. The very contrary is the truth.
law-author: William Stanley Jevons
law-url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jevons_paradox
law-year: 1865
---

Named after the economist William Stanley Jevons, this paradox observes that technological progress which makes a resource cheaper or more efficient to use tends to increase, rather than decrease, the overall consumption of that resource. Jevons first described this in his book *The Coal Question*, arguing that more efficient steam engines would not save coal but instead spur its wider adoption and, ultimately, greater total use. The effect is also known as the rebound effect. For software, the paradox offers a cautionary counterpoint to Moore's Law: as computation becomes cheaper and faster, demand for it tends to grow to fill, and even exceed, the newly available capacity.
